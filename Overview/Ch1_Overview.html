What Are Employee Benefits?

Employee benefits = compensation other than an hourly wage or salary.

Examples include health care, paid time off, retirement plans, disability and life insurance, educational assistance, and wellness or family assistance programs.

We can organize benefits according to the role they serve and the source of the benefit.

Three Roles of Benefits

1. Protection

Protect employees and families from income loss, illness, or disability.

2. Paid Time Off

Compensate employees when they are not performing their normal work duties.

Examples: vacation, sick leave, holidays.

3. Accommodation & Enhancement

Support employees and families through programs such as wellness, flexible work arrangements, educational assistance, and family assistance.

Two Sources of Benefits

Legally Required

Benefits required by federal, state, or local government.

  • Social Security
  • Workers’ compensation
  • Family and Medical Leave Act
  • Certain paid-leave requirements
  • Other legally required programs

Discretionary

Benefits employers choose to provide beyond legal requirements.

  • Health and dental benefits
  • Retirement plans
  • Life insurance
  • Paid time off
  • Educational assistance

Keep this distinction in mind throughout the course.

The Employee-Benefits Field

Benefits professionals may:

  • Design and manage benefit programs
  • Coordinate retirement plans
  • Manage health-care benefits
  • Work with HR professionals and managers
  • Help organizations comply with laws
  • Support recruitment and retention

Benefits are both an HR function and a business decision.

Two Strategic Approaches

Top-Down — Proactive

  • Begin with organizational strategy
  • Regularly review benefits
  • Anticipate future needs
  • Adjust the program strategically

Backing-In — Reactive

  • A problem occurs
  • Organization investigates
  • Benefits are changed in response

Think: Plan ahead vs. respond to a problem.

What Information Do Employers Need?

External Environment

  • Industry prospects
  • Economic conditions
  • Employer benefit costs
  • Government regulation
  • Labor-force demographics

Internal Environment

  • Workforce demographics
  • Employee needs and preferences
  • Collective bargaining agreements

Benefits planning requires information about both the organization and its environment.
